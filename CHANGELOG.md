## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Details for invoices, the second part of invoicing: the form of a customer (*New customer* and *Edit customer*) has two new optional fields, **Address for invoices** (up to 6 lines; German: *Adresse für Rechnungen*) and **Tax ID** (German: *Steuernummer*), and a customer that has them shows them under its email. In **Settings**, the group **Invoicing** has the new field **Bank details** (German: *Bankverbindung*, up to 4 lines) for your IBAN, BIC and bank. They are stored for the invoice, which follows in a later version; reports do not print them, and nothing else changes. Existing installs: back up, then apply the migration with `kubectl -n time-tracking exec -i deploy/postgres -- sh -c 'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB"' < backend/migrations/016_invoice_details.sql`, rebuild the images and update with `./k8s/update-images.sh`.
- Settings for invoices, the first part of invoicing: in **Settings**, the new group **Invoicing** (German: *Rechnungen*) has **VAT** (*Regular, with VAT* or *Small business, no VAT (§19 UStG)*), the **VAT rate** (19 %, 7 % or 0 %), the **Invoice number pattern** and the **Next invoice number**. The pattern is text with `{YYYY}` (year), `{YY}`, `{MM}` (month) and one counter such as `{NNNN}`, for example `{YYYY}-{NNNN}` for `2026-0001`; a live preview shows the next number. If the pattern has a year, the counter starts again at 1 every year; otherwise it keeps counting. The counter can only be raised, so a number is never used twice. Your **Tax ID** (*Steuernummer*) is in **Your details**. Nothing uses these settings yet, because invoices follow in a later version, so nothing changes for you until then. Existing installs: back up, then apply the migration with `kubectl -n time-tracking exec -i deploy/postgres -- sh -c 'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB"' < backend/migrations/015_settings_invoicing.sql`, rebuild the images and update with `./k8s/update-images.sh`.
- Your logo on the PDF report: in **Settings**, group **Your details**, the new field **Logo** (German: *Logo*) takes a PNG or JPEG of up to 1 MB and 2000 × 2000 pixels. It is stored as soon as you choose it, shows a preview, and **Remove logo** removes it again. The PDF report prints it at the top left, with your name, address and email to its right; without a logo nothing changes, and the CSV is never affected. SVG files are refused. The tool needs a larger upload limit for this: the cluster's ingress and the frontend's nginx now accept 2 MB for the logo upload only. Existing installs: back up, then apply the migration with `kubectl -n time-tracking exec -i deploy/postgres -- sh -c 'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB"' < backend/migrations/014_sender_logo.sql`, apply the changed ingress with `kubectl apply -f k8s/ingress.yaml`, rebuild the images and update with `./k8s/update-images.sh`.
- Your own details on the PDF report: in **Settings**, the new group **Your details** (German: *Deine Angaben*) has **Your name or business name**, **Address** (up to 6 lines) and **Email address**. If you fill them in, every PDF report starts with them at the top right, so it can go to a customer as it is. Leave them empty and the report looks as before. The CSV and the screen do not change. A logo and a tax ID follow in later changes. Existing installs: back up, then apply the migration with `kubectl -n time-tracking exec -i deploy/postgres -- sh -c 'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB"' < backend/migrations/013_settings_sender_details.sql`, rebuild the images and update with `./k8s/update-images.sh`.
- Choose the first day of the week: in **Settings**, the new group **Calendar** has **First day of the week** (German: *Erster Tag der Woche*), **Monday** (the default) or **Sunday**. The Dashboard's *This week* figure (it names the first day now), the **Weeks** bars and the day labels of the hours chart, and the period choices *This week* and *Last week* on the Entries page follow it, and a saved change applies without a reload. A week from Sunday is numbered like the ISO week that holds most of its days (the one of its Monday). Months and reports stay calendar months. Existing installs: back up, then apply the migration with `kubectl -n time-tracking exec -i deploy/postgres -- sh -c 'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB"' < backend/migrations/012_settings_week_start.sql`, rebuild the images and update with `./k8s/update-images.sh`.
- Resume from the Dashboard and the Entries page: the **Resume** button (German: *Fortsetzen*) is now also beside each entry in **Recent entries** on the Dashboard and before **Open** on each finished result on the **Entries** page, so you can pick up an activity from wherever you find it, without opening Time tracking first. It works as on Time tracking: the same entry runs on, and the time since its end counts as work time. The button is disabled while a timer is running. You stay on the page, the running timer shows in the header, and focus moves to its **Stop timer** button. The Entries results reload, so the resumed entry shows as running.
- Resume an entry: on the Time tracking page, a finished entry has a new **Resume** button (German: *Fortsetzen*), the play icon before Edit. It runs that entry on again as the running timer, with its project, description and original start, so after a call you no longer select and type them again and the activity stays one entry. **The time between the end of the entry and the resume counts as work time**; after a longer break start a new entry instead. While a timer is running, the button is disabled. After resuming, focus moves to the **Stop** button. No migration needed; rebuild the images and update with `./k8s/update-images.sh`.
- A default hourly rate: in **Settings**, group **Time and billing**, **Default hourly rate for new projects (€)** (German: *Standard-Stundensatz für neue Projekte (€)*). The form for a new project starts with this rate, and you can still change it for each project. Leave it empty for no default, as before. The rate is typed in the format of the language (`80,00` in German, `80.00` in English); anything else shows an error next to the field and nothing is saved. Existing projects and the amounts of existing reports do not change. Existing installs: back up, then apply the migration with `kubectl -n time-tracking exec -i deploy/postgres -- sh -c 'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB"' < backend/migrations/010_settings_default_hourly_rate.sql`, rebuild the images and update with `./k8s/update-images.sh`.
- A report language per customer: in the form of a customer (*New customer* and *Edit customer*) the new select **Report language** (German: *Berichtssprache*) offers **Same as the app** (the default, so nothing changes for existing customers), **German** and **English**. The PDF and the CSV of a customer with its own report language always come out in that language, whatever language the app is in: texts, column titles, month names, number and date formats, the CSV delimiter and the file name. A customer with German and one with English reports no longer needs the app switched before each download. On the Reports page a line under the download buttons says which language the files will use; the page and the report on screen keep following the app language. Existing installs: back up, then apply the migration with `kubectl -n time-tracking exec -i deploy/postgres -- sh -c 'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB"' < backend/migrations/011_customer_report_language.sql`, rebuild the images and update with `./k8s/update-images.sh`.
- Developers: `./k8s/update-images.sh` updates the images on your cluster in one step. After building them, it imports both into the node, restarts the backend and the frontend, waits for the rollout and then removes the previous copies of the two images from the node, which every rebuild used to leave behind (about 290 MB each) until the shared node's disk was full and the database could not start. Images of other apps are never touched, and a failed rollout keeps the old images for a rollback. `k8s/README.md` explains how to check the free space of the node and what to do when it is nearly full.
- Local overrides for the API's allowed origins: if you open the tool at another address on your own machine (for example `https://time-tracking.local:8443`), put `CORS_ORIGIN=...` in the gitignored `k8s/.env.local` and load it into the optional ConfigMap `time-tracking-local`, instead of editing `k8s/configmap.yaml`. Nothing changes without it, see "Local Overrides" in `k8s/README.md`.
- A rounding rule per customer: in the form of a customer (*New customer* and *Edit customer*) the new select **Rounding** (German: *Rundung*) offers **Use the global setting** (the default, so nothing changes for existing customers), **No rounding**, or a step (5 minutes, 10 minutes, quarter hour, half hour, hour) rounded **up** or **to the nearest**. The customer's rule wins over the global setting in its reports (screen, PDF, CSV), on the Dashboard and in the month summary: a customer can be rounded to a half hour while another one is billed exactly. A customer with its own rule shows it under its name. When the customers of the month do not share one rule, the notes on the Dashboard say *Rounding differs by customer.* and each customer's report names its own rule. Existing installs: back up, then apply the migration with `kubectl -n time-tracking exec -i deploy/postgres -- sh -c 'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB"' < backend/migrations/009_customer_rounding.sql`, rebuild the images and restart the backend and the frontend.
- More rounding steps: **Round time entries** (German: *Zeiteinträge runden*) in the settings now also offers **5 minutes**, **10 minutes** and **Hour (60 minutes)** next to quarter and half hour. Reports show time as hours and minutes (see below), so a step of 5 or 10 minutes is exact there: twelve entries of 5 minutes add up to `1:00 h`, six of 10 minutes too; only the CSV, which has decimal hours, prints 5 minutes as 0,08 and 10 minutes as 0,17. The report sentence names the new steps too (*Hours rounded up to full hours.*, *Hours rounded to the nearest 10 minutes.*). Existing installs: back up, then apply the migration (after `007_settings_rounding.sql`, which it needs) with `kubectl -n time-tracking exec -i deploy/postgres -- sh -c 'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB"' < backend/migrations/008_settings_rounding_steps.sql`, rebuild the images and restart the backend and the frontend.
- Rounding of tracked time in reports: in **Settings**, group **Time and billing**, **Round time entries** (German: *Zeiteinträge runden*) rounds the hours of each entry to a **quarter hour** or a **half hour**, with **Direction** (*Richtung*) **Up** (the default) or **To the nearest**. Each entry is rounded on its own; an entry never counts less than one step; stored entries are never changed. The Reports page, the PDF and the CSV show the rounded hours, the amounts are calculated from them (time × rate equals the amount on every row), and, if you switch on **Name the rounding rule in reports** (*Rundungsregel in Berichten nennen*, off by default), the Reports page and the PDF say which rule was applied (*Hours rounded up to quarter hours.*). The CSV has the rounded hours without a note and keeps the exact start and end times. The setting also applies to reports of past months; files you have already downloaded do not change. The default is **No rounding**, so nothing changes until you choose a step. The Dashboard's billing figures (the billable amount, *By customer* and *Billing*) and the month summary on the Time tracking page use the rounded hours too, so they equal the report; each of those cards names the rule. The tracked time stays exact: the timer, the entry lists, the Entries search, and on the Dashboard *This week*, *This month*, the chart and the recent entries. Existing installs: back up, then apply the migration with `kubectl -n time-tracking exec -i deploy/postgres -- sh -c 'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB"' < backend/migrations/007_settings_rounding.sql`, rebuild the images and restart the backend and the frontend.
- The first setting, in the group **Time and billing** (German: *Zeit und Abrechnung*): **Show hourly rate and amounts in reports** (German: *Stundensatz und Beträge in Berichten anzeigen*). It is off by default, so reports look as before. The Reports page starts from it in every browser; its checkbox *Show hourly rate and amounts* still changes only the report you are looking at, with its PDF and CSV downloads, for that visit. If the settings cannot be loaded, the Reports page shows an error with *Try again* and starts without amounts.
- The Settings page can hold options in named groups and saves all of them with one **Save** button (German: *Speichern*): it confirms *Settings saved.*, and when saving fails it shows an error and keeps what you typed. The settings load once when the tool opens, so a saved change applies on every page without a reload.
- New **Settings** page (German: *Einstellungen*) at the end of the main navigation, at `/settings`, for options that apply to the whole tool in every browser. Existing installs: back up, then apply the two migrations with `for f in 005_settings 006_settings_show_amounts; do kubectl -n time-tracking exec -i deploy/postgres -- sh -c 'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB"' < backend/migrations/$f.sql; done`, rebuild the images and restart the backend and the frontend.
- Monthly reports can include the hourly rate and the amount per project and the total amount, in the PDF (an amount column, a block *Per project* and a *TOTAL AMOUNT* line), in the CSV (two columns, *Rate (EUR/h)* and *Amount (EUR)*) and in the report data. They are off unless asked for: without the option every report looks exactly as before. Amounts are in euros; each entry's hours are rounded to hundredths and its amount to cents, so the printed numbers add up exactly. On the Reports page, the checkbox *Show hourly rate and amounts* turns them on.
- Every page has its own address (`/`, `/time-tracking`, `/entries`, `/reports`): a reload keeps the page, the browser's back and forward buttons move between pages, and you can bookmark a page or open it in a new tab (also with a middle click on the navigation). A link opened while logged out leads to the login and then to that page. An unknown address shows a *Page not found* page with a link to the Dashboard. The browser tab's title names the page, and focus moves to the new page's heading for keyboard and screen reader users.
- The selected customer, project and month are part of the address too (`/time-tracking/3/7`, `/reports/3/2026-09`): a reload, a bookmark or a link you send keeps them, and Back returns to the customer you had open before. The places that open another page with a selection (Dashboard, *Open report*, *Open* on the Entries page) are real links now, so "open in a new tab" works. An address for a customer or project that does not exist (any more) says so and links back. On the Reports page, the month arrows wait until a customer is chosen.
- New **Entries** page (German: *Einträge*) between *Time tracking* and *Reports*: search the descriptions of all customers and filter by customer, project and period (today, this week, last month, a custom range and more), sorted newest or oldest first, with the number of entries and hours found. It opens with the current month; *Reset filters* clears every filter. The filters can be collapsed and the active ones stay visible as chips you can remove. A result opens the customer's page in *Time tracking*.
- Reports can be downloaded as a CSV file for spreadsheets: **Download CSV** (German: *CSV herunterladen*) on the Reports page next to *Download PDF* lists the same entries of the customer and month with date, start, end, customer, project, description and hours. German files use `;` between the columns and decimal commas, English files use `,` and decimal points, so the file opens correctly in Excel in the matching language. With the amounts option (see above) the file also has the hourly rate and the amount of each entry.
- Login: the tool asks for an email and a password and has a *Log out* button in the header. There is one user; create it (and change the password later) with `node src/scripts/set-user.js you@example.com`, which asks for the password: at least 10 characters with a capital letter, a number and a special character. You stay logged in for up to 30 days, or until you have not used the tool for 14 days. After 10 failed logins the login is blocked for up to 15 minutes. Existing installs: see "Upgrading an Existing Install to the Login" in `k8s/README.md`.
- The tool is served over HTTPS at https://time-tracking.local; http redirects to it. `k8s/setup-tls.sh` creates a certificate your browsers trust (needs `mkcert`). Existing installs run it once and restart the backend, see "Upgrading an Existing Install to HTTPS" in `k8s/README.md`.
- Tooling: every backend and frontend test run ends with a timing report: time per test file, the 10 slowest tests (`TEST_TIMINGS=all` for every test) and the time of the entire run.

### Changed

- Developers: the Vite dev server (`npm run dev`) listens on `localhost` only, so other devices on the network can no longer open it. To reach it from another device or from a container on purpose, start it with `npm run dev -- --host 0.0.0.0`. The tool in the cluster is not affected.
- Durations on the Reports page and in the PDF report are shown as hours and minutes too (`3:59 h`, in rows, project lines and the total), and amounts are calculated from the same whole minutes: rate × minutes / 60, rounded to cents for each entry, so time × rate equals the amount on every row. **Amounts can differ by a few cents from before**, also when you generate an earlier month again (1:05 h at 95,00 €/h is now 102,92 € instead of 102,60 €, which was calculated from the printed 1,08 h); files you have already downloaded do not change. The Dashboard and the month summary use the same rule, so their amounts equal the report even without a rounding rule. With a rounding step of 5 or 10 minutes, six entries of 10 minutes (or twelve of 5) now add up to exactly `1:00 h` in the report. The CSV download keeps decimal hours in its Hours column so a spreadsheet can add them up (each is the entry's whole minutes divided by 60). The report data of the API stays decimal. Nothing in your data changes.
- Durations are shown as hours and minutes: `3:59 h` instead of `3,98 h` on the Dashboard (this week, this month, the average per day, the chart and its table, *By customer*, *Billing*, recent entries, the message after stopping the timer), on the Time tracking page (entries, the total of each project, the month summary) and on the Entries page. Each entry counts whole minutes and totals add up those minutes, so the rows always add up exactly to their total; with a rounding rule of 5 or 10 minutes, six entries of 10 minutes show `1:00 h`. The CSV still shows decimal hours. Nothing in your data changes.
- Tooling: the documentation chapters for the Dashboard, Time tracking and Reports now show a screenshot of the page (`documentation/ui/images/`); the browser review chapter explains how to take them, and a test checks that every linked image exists and has an alt text.
- Tooling: the router tests wait for the address, the page title and the focus instead of checking them at once, so they no longer fail at random.
- The Reports page no longer shows hourly rates and amounts by default; tick *Show hourly rate and amounts* to see them again, also in the PDF and CSV downloads. The choice the browser used to remember is gone: the default is now a setting (see above), and the old stored choice is removed from your browser on the next visit of the Reports page.
- The backend and the database can no longer be reached directly from other devices or other pods; Docker Compose publishes only the web page, and only on this machine (development ports: see the README). Anyone who can open the tool can still use it, and the login protects the data. Existing installs: see "Network Policy" in `k8s/README.md`.
- Only the tool's own backend can connect to the database in the cluster; backups are not affected. Existing installs: see "Network Policy" in `k8s/README.md`.
- Tooling: the frontend test run prints its timing report again after the Vitest 5 update.
- Tooling: changes reach `main` through pull requests; `main` is protected and needs the passing checks `backend tests` and `frontend tests`.
- Tooling: CodeQL code scanning is on for the repository.
- Tooling: `CLAUDE.md` is reordered into Things worth knowing, Commands, Layout, Styling, Kubernetes, Testing and Rules, and the rules are merged into groups with the pull request process as a numbered list.
- Tooling: new documentation chapter "Browser review": how to review a change in the browser with a throwaway database and sample data, also for branches in a git worktree. A test checks that every documentation chapter is linked from the index.
- Screen readers now read each project's and customer's share of the month's hours (e.g. "70 % of total") next to the hours in the month summary and in "By customer" on the Dashboard.
- Tooling: the repository has a Code of Conduct (Contributor Covenant 2.1).
- Tooling: fixed a test of `k8s/setup-tls.sh` that failed at random; its fake `openssl` now reads its input like the real one.
- The API answers `401` without a login for everything except `/health`. Anything else that calls it, such as scripts, has to log in first (`POST /api/auth/login`) and send the cookie.

### Fixed

- Restoring a backup (`k8s/restore-db.sh`) can no longer report success after the data stream was cut off on the way into the cluster. Before, a cut that fell between two statements committed a partial restore (for example empty tables) and still printed "Restored". Now the restore commits only if the whole dump arrived; otherwise it fails with a message and changes nothing, and after a successful restore it prints the number of rows per table, so you see what came back. No migration or image rebuild needed.
- On the Time tracking page, the month summary no longer prints a large billable amount over the number of entries: the three figures take the space they need and wrap onto a new line on narrow screens.
- Stopping the timer can no longer save an entry that ends before it starts (for example when the browser's clock is behind): the server's time is used instead. Entries that already end before they start can be corrected by editing them (the edit form now opens them with the end on the day of the start).
- Creating a project for a customer that does not exist now answers `400` ("Customer not found") instead of a server error `500`. The same goes for a customer id that is not a number.
- With a remote database, the backend now starts: it waits for the database in `DATABASE_URL` instead of always for the in-cluster Postgres.
- The arrows of the dropdowns (project, customer and status selects, forms) now have space to the right border, in the light and the dark theme.
